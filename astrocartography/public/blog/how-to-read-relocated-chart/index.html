How to read a relocated chart, step by step

To read a relocated chart, keep the natal planets, signs and planet-to-planet aspects fixed, then study what the new location changes: the Ascendant, Midheaven, four angles, house cusps, house placements and aspects to the new angles. Start with planets within a tight orb of AC, DC, MC or IC; identify the two or three houses that gain emphasis; then compare those changes with the natal chart and with another viable city. The chart describes a shift of emphasis in astrological tradition—not a new personality, a guaranteed event or scientific forecast.

The short answer

A relocated chart is a natal chart recalculated for a different geographic location while preserving the original birth instant; its planetary signs and interplanetary aspects remain fixed while its angles and houses change.

The shortest useful reading is: new angles → angular planets → changed houses → repeated natal themes → comparison with lived reality. Do not interpret all twelve houses at equal volume, and never choose a city from astrology alone.

First, know exactly what changes—and what does not

A relocated chart is the birth chart calculated for the same instant but viewed from another place on Earth. Date and instant remain fixed. The target city's latitude and longitude replace the birthplace coordinates, so the local horizon and meridian change. In chart language, that redraws the Ascendant–Descendant axis, the Midheaven–IC axis and the houses built around them.

The planets do not jump to new zodiac signs. Their degrees and their aspects to one another remain essentially the same because the underlying moment has not changed. Astrodienst's reference on relocated chart basics states the same calculation rule: replace the place, not the time; planets keep their degrees while angles and houses change. Its relocation astrology reference also distinguishes the stable planetary configuration from location-dependent house division.

This makes a relocated chart a change of stage, not a replacement cast. A natal Saturn–Moon square remains a Saturn–Moon square; if Saturn becomes conjunct the relocated Midheaven, that existing pattern may receive more public or vocational emphasis in traditional interpretation. The new chart cannot delete a natal difficulty or manufacture a trait that was never present. It reorganizes where the established chart is most visible.

Before interpreting: verify time, timezone and city

A relocation interpretation can only be as precise as its inputs. Use the recorded birth time, not a time converted to the target city's clock. The software should translate the original local birth time into a single universal instant, then calculate the target city's local horizon for that instant. Historical daylight-saving rules matter. The IANA Time Zone Database exists specifically to represent the history of local civil time and is updated when political time-zone rules change; it validates the time conversion layer, not astrology.

Record the source of the birth time: timed certificate or hospital record, written family record, or approximate memory. If it is approximate, calculate the target city at the earliest and latest plausible times. A useful interpretation should survive that sensitivity check. If a planet is angular at 08:00 but far from the angle at 08:20, label the conclusion fragile. The guide to astrocartography without an exact birth time explains why noon charts and guessed times cannot support confident angular claims.

Use the coordinates of the city you are actually considering. A metropolitan center is normally sufficient for a broad reading, but do not compare “California” with “Portugal.” A relocation chart is location-specific. Keep the same house system when comparing cities so a settings change is not mistaken for a geographic change. Save a calculation note with birth-time source, timezone resolution, coordinates, house system and software date; this small provenance record makes later comparisons reproducible.

The seven-step reading order

The most common error is reading a relocated wheel as though it were a second natal chart and giving every symbol equal weight. A better method is hierarchical. Each step earns the next one.

  1. Write down the new four angles. Record relocated Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven and IC by sign and degree. These establish the local frame.
  2. Find planets closest to an angle. Start with conjunctions to AC, DC, MC or IC. Record the actual angular distance rather than calling every planet in an angular house “on the angle.”
  3. Identify the largest house changes. Note planets that move into or out of the 1st, 4th, 7th or 10th houses, then any cluster that changes life area.
  4. Carry the natal condition forward. A relocated Jupiter is still your natal Jupiter, with the same sign and aspects. Read its existing strengths and tensions before adding the new house.
  5. Check new aspects to the angles. A planet can form a close conjunction, opposition, square or trine to a relocated angle even though planet-to-planet aspects stay fixed. Prioritize conjunctions; label wider or minor aspects as secondary conventions.
  6. Look for repetition across layers. A nearby Jupiter-MC line, Jupiter near the relocated MC and Jupiter ruling a newly emphasized house repeat one theme. Repetition is more informative than stretching one distant line.
  7. Compare, calibrate and reality-check. Put two feasible cities side by side, test a place you already know, and let housing, safety, work, health, relationships, law and budget retain veto power.

How to interpret each relocated angle

The four angles are not interchangeable. They tell you where an angular planet's symbolism is traditionally expected to become most noticeable:

Relocated angleTraditional focusFirst question to ask
Ascendant (AC)Identity, body, approach, visibility, new beginningsHow might I present myself and initiate life here?
Descendant (DC)Partnership, clients, collaborators, projection and conflictWhich kinds of one-to-one dynamics receive more attention?
Midheaven (MC)Career, public role, reputation, direction and authorityWhat becomes visible or consequential in public life?
Imum Coeli (IC)Home, privacy, roots, family, belonging and inner baseWhat kind of foundation might I build or revisit?

Planet plus angle creates the complete phrase. Venus on the relocated MC is not simply “a Venus place”; tradition reads Venusian themes through work, reputation and public connection. Venus on the relocated IC points instead toward private comfort, home aesthetics and belonging. The same structure applies to every planet. Our guides to the Ascendant, Descendant, Midheaven and IC explain the four lenses in depth.

A planet near an angle normally outranks a generic house change because the map's core technique is angularity. But “near” must be disclosed. There is no scientifically validated astrological orb. Use a consistent convention, show the degree distance and compare tighter with wider rather than pretending an invisible boundary has been measured. A planet 0°40′ from the MC is a clearer angular statement than one 8° away.

Read changed houses without erasing the natal chart

After the angles, compare natal and relocated house placements. Look for changes that simplify into a useful sentence: “Natal Mercury in the 12th moves to the relocated 10th,” or “a natal 2nd-house cluster becomes a relocated 7th-house cluster.” Then translate it cautiously: the place may invite the established Mercury pattern into more public or vocational contexts, or bring the cluster's concerns into partnership and client work.

Three guardrails keep this step from becoming deterministic:

  • Keep both charts visible. The natal chart remains the base; the relocated chart is a location layer.
  • Prioritize clusters and angular houses. Do not write twelve equal paragraphs. Focus on the 1st, 4th, 7th and 10th, close angle contacts, and houses gaining several planets.
  • Read rulers as supporting context. A relocated Ascendant ruler can describe how the local presentation is organized, but it should not outweigh a planet sitting almost exactly on the Midheaven.

House systems can produce different intermediate cusps at high latitudes or near sign boundaries. If your conclusion flips when you change systems, report that sensitivity instead of selecting the wheel you prefer. The angular axes themselves remain the most stable starting point.

The responsible reading does not declare City A “better for career” and City B “better for love.” It produces two hypotheses. City A strongly repeats Mercury through the MC and 10th house; the natal Saturn trine suggests that structure, editing, research or sustained communication belongs in the interpretation. City B strongly repeats Venus through IC and 4th house, but the natal Venus–Neptune square warns against turning comfort or belonging into idealization. Those are reflective prompts to test against job terms, legal status, partner expectations and actual time spent in each place.

If the work visa makes City B impossible, astrology does not overrule immigration law. If City A's role is compelling but the support deficit matters, the chart can help name the trade-off; it cannot decide your tolerance for it. This is why the best comparison begins with a viable shortlist and ends with a practical matrix.

How the relocated chart connects to astrocartography

Astrocartography and the relocated chart calculate the same location principle in different formats. The map shows where each natal planet was on one of the four angles around the world. A relocated wheel shows the complete house structure for one chosen city. If a city lies exactly on the natal Jupiter-MC line, Jupiter should be on or extremely close to the Midheaven in that city's relocated chart, subject to consistent settings and calculation method.

Use the map for breadth and the wheel for depth:

  • Scan globally with the astrocartography calculator.
  • Measure the real proximity using the line-distance guide, not the visual gap to a label.
  • Shortlist only viable places, including line-light candidates; no major line nearby is not a failed map.
  • Cast relocated charts for two or three finalists and use the seven-step reading order above.

A line-light city may still have meaningful house changes, a close aspect to a relocated angle or a relevant paran. Conversely, a city on a visually appealing line can reveal an uncomfortable natal pattern moving onto an angle. The wheel does not contradict the map; it supplies information the map does not display.

Eight mistakes that produce confident but weak readings

  1. Changing the time zone to the target city. Keep the birth instant; change the viewing coordinates.
  2. Reading the relocated chart without the natal chart. The natal condition of each planet travels with you.
  3. Calling a whole country one relocation. Angles and cusps are calculated for coordinates, not national identity.
  4. Treating every house move as equally important. Angularity, clusters and clear changes deserve priority.
  5. Using an undisclosed orb. State the degree distance; do not turn a loose contact into an exact one.
  6. Assuming a new Ascendant creates a new personality. It is traditionally read as a location-specific mode of presentation, not a replacement identity.
  7. Choosing only “benefic” planets. Venus and Jupiter can carry natal tensions; Saturn and Mars can symbolize useful structure or initiative. Context outranks labels.
  8. Letting the chart overrule facts. A wheel cannot evaluate a lease, doctor, labor market, visa, discrimination risk or support network.

A ninth, subtler mistake is interpreting after you already know which city you want. This invites selective attention. Record the chart features first, using the comparison worksheet, then add city names and practical facts. Where possible, test the same method on a former home before applying it to an unknown future.

Evidence boundary: exact calculation does not prove astrological effects

The calculation layer has technical components that can be checked: coordinates, historical civil time, the conversion to a universal instant, Earth rotation, planetary positions and the local horizon. Authoritative resources such as IANA's time-zone data and the International Astronomical Union's Standards of Fundamental Astronomy service support parts of that astronomy and timekeeping infrastructure.

They do not validate the astrological claim that a relocated angular planet changes life outcomes. Astrology has not been scientifically established. Shawn Carlson's well-known double-blind natal astrology test in Nature did not test relocated charts specifically, so it should not be misrepresented as a direct relocation experiment; it belongs here only as part of the broader evidence boundary. There is no validated universal orb, city score or “best place” formula.

The intellectually honest use is reflective and comparative: make the calculation reproducible, label the interpretation as tradition, test it against places you know, record uncertainty, and keep decisions multi-factor. Frequently asked questions

What is a relocated chart?

A relocated chart is a natal chart calculated for the same birth instant but viewed from another location. Planet signs, degrees and planet-to-planet aspects stay the same; the Ascendant, Midheaven, other angles, house cusps and house placements change with the target city's coordinates.

How do I read a relocated chart?

Read it in this order: record the new AC, DC, MC and IC; find planets closest to those angles; identify the largest house changes; carry each planet's natal sign and aspects forward; check new aspects to angles; look for repetition with astrocartography; then compare the result with lived and practical reality.

Does your rising sign change in a relocated chart?

The relocated Ascendant can be in a different sign because the local eastern horizon changes with latitude and longitude. Astrological tradition reads that as a location-specific emphasis in presentation and approach, not as a replacement for your natal Ascendant or personality.

What stays the same in a relocation chart?

The birth instant remains fixed, so the planets keep their zodiac signs and degrees and keep their aspects to one another. What changes is the location-dependent frame: angles, house cusps, planet houses and aspects from planets to those new angles.

Which chart is more important: natal or relocated?

The natal chart remains the base. A relocated chart is a location layer that emphasizes parts of the natal pattern through new angles and houses. Read both together; do not use the relocated wheel to cancel natal placements or invent a different personality.

What is the difference between a relocated chart and astrocartography?

Astrocartography is a global map showing where natal planets were angular; a relocated chart is a full house-and-angle wheel for one city. Use the map to discover or compare regions, then use relocated charts to study a small shortlist in depth.

Do I use the target city's time zone when relocating my chart?

No. Preserve the actual birth instant. Reliable software resolves the historical timezone of the birthplace, converts that local birth time to a universal instant, and calculates the target city's horizon for that same instant. You change coordinates, not the event.

Can a relocated chart tell me where I should move?

It can structure a reflective comparison, but it cannot establish which city will produce a good outcome. Relocation astrology is not scientifically validated. Compare only viable cities and let safety, law, work, cost, health, housing, climate and relationships retain final authority.
