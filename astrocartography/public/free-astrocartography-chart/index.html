Creating your own map

Once you want to see your own lines, you need three pieces of information, and accuracy matters most for the third:

  • Birth date — the day you were born.
  • Exact birth time — ideally to the minute, from a birth certificate or hospital record. Time is what fixes the four angles (ASC, DSC, MC, IC), and the angular lines move noticeably even with a small error. If your time is uncertain, the planetary positions are still meaningful, but the angle-based lines should be read more loosely.
  • Birth place — the city and country, which sets the coordinates the chart is calculated from.

Enter those and Natal Navigator computes your personal map: your own forty lines on the same 3D globe, your own nearest cities, your own natal wheel. It is the identical interface you already learned in the demo, now drawn from your life rather than a famous one.
