How the calculator works

  1. Enter your birth date. The calendar accepts any year — historical births are fully supported.
  2. Enter your exact birth time. As precise as possible. Every four minutes shifts your Ascendant and Descendant lines by roughly one degree of longitude.
  3. Search for your birth city. We auto-resolve latitude, longitude and historical time zone (including DST in force on that date).
  4. View your globe. All 40 lines — 10 planets × 4 angles — render on a rotating 3D Earth, color-coded as thrive, neutral or caution zones.

How accurate is the calculator?

Natal Navigator uses the open-source astronomy-engine library, the same engine used by planetarium software and astronomical research tools. Planetary positions are computed to sub-arcsecond precision. The Ascendant and Descendant use an analytical solver rather than brute-force approximation, so our lines are as geometrically exact as the input birth time allows.

This means the only source of uncertainty in your personal map is the accuracy of your birth time. A certified birth certificate usually lists the minute; some list the hour only, in which case results should be treated as approximate.

What if I don’t know my exact birth time?

You can still run the calculator, but be aware: the MC/IC lines move about 15 degrees of longitude per hour of time difference, and ASC/DC can move even faster near the poles. Treat the output as directional rather than precise.
