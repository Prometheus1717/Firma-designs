How accurate is the calculator?

Natal Navigator uses the open-source astronomy-engine library, the same engine used by planetarium software and astronomical research tools. Planetary positions are computed to sub-arcsecond precision. The Ascendant and Descendant use an analytical solver rather than brute-force approximation.

The only source of uncertainty in your personal map is the accuracy of your birth time. A certified birth certificate usually lists the minute; some list the hour only, in which case results should be treated as approximate.

Three inputs: date of birth, exact time of birth, and birth city. Time zone, coordinates, planetary positions, and line paths are all resolved automatically.

What if I don’t know my exact birth time?

You can still run the calculator, but the MC/IC lines move about 15 degrees per hour of time difference, and ASC/DC can move even faster near the poles. Treat the output as directional rather than precise.

Birth data linked to a free account is stored securely and encrypted at rest. Demo-mode input is never persisted. You can delete your account and its data at any time.
